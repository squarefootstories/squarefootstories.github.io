Tiny Kitchen Counter Space Hacks: 7 Ways to Free Up Room

By the Square Foot Stories team · Updated October 10, 2026 · 1 min read · How we write guides

When a kitchen has almost no counter, every flat surface counts. These seven ideas create prep space over the sink, on the stove and up the walls.

Over-the-sink dish rack

A rack that sits over the sink drains dishes straight into it and frees the counter where a dish rack usually sits.

Stovetop cover board

A board that covers unused burners becomes extra prep space when you're not cooking. Never use it while the stove is on.

Wall rail with hooks

A rail under the cabinets holds utensils, mugs and towels so they're off the counter.

Fold-down wall table

A drop-leaf table on a free wall adds a prep or dining surface that folds away.

Appliances on a cart

Keep the microwave or coffee maker on a rolling cart instead of the counter.

Corner lazy Susan

A turntable makes awkward corner space useful for oils, sauces and spices.

Mugs hung under shelves

Under-shelf hooks or baskets store mugs and small items in space that's usually empty.

What to look for before you buy

Over-the-sink racks

Measure the sink width and depth. Adjustable racks fit more sinks.

Stovetop covers

Only use covers when the stove is off and cool, and check they fit your stove size.

Fold-down tables

Check the weight rating and wall mounting needs.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Leaving every appliance on the counter.
  • Using a stovetop cover while burners are hot.
  • Buying racks without measuring the sink.

Questions people ask

How can I get more counter space in a tiny kitchen?

Use the sink and stove for temporary surfaces, a rolling cart or fold-down table for extra work space, and move appliances off the counter.

Where do I put appliances in a small kitchen?

Keep only daily ones out. Store others on a cart, a high shelf or in a cabinet.

Clear the counter completely, then only put back what you use every day.
