Wool vs Fleece Throw Blanket: Which Is Better for a Cozy Home?

By the Square Foot Stories team · Updated October 10, 2026 · 2 min read · How we write guides

A throw blanket is one of the easiest ways to make a small living room or bedroom feel cozy. The two most common materials, wool and fleece, feel and behave quite differently. Here is how they compare.

Wool: the case for it

Wool is warm, breathes well and handles a range of temperatures, so it is less likely to leave you sweaty. It is a natural fiber and a good wool throw can last for many years.

Wool: the trade-offs

Some wool feels scratchy, especially against bare skin. It usually costs more than fleece, and many wool throws need a gentle or wool cycle, or hand washing.

Fleece: the case for it

Fleece is soft, light and inexpensive. Most fleece throws go straight into the washing machine and dry quickly, which suits homes with pets or kids.

Fleece: the trade-offs

Fleece is usually made from polyester, so it breathes less than wool and can feel warm in a stuffy room. It can pill over time and it sheds tiny synthetic fibers when washed.

Quick verdict: Want lasting warmth and a natural fiber? Choose wool. Want something soft, cheap and easy to wash? Choose fleece.

What to look for before you buy

For wool

Merino and lambswool tend to feel softer than coarser wools. Read the care label to see whether it is machine washable before you buy.

For fleece

Thicker, denser fleece usually pills less than very thin fleece. Look for a neat edge finish that will not unravel in the wash.

Size

A throw around 50 by 60 inches covers one adult on a sofa. Bigger throws look heavy on a small loveseat.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Buying a scratchy wool throw for bare-skin use without feeling it first.
  • Washing wool on a hot cycle, which can shrink it.
  • Choosing a throw so large it swamps a small sofa.

Questions people ask

Is wool or fleece warmer?

Both can be warm. Wool tends to regulate temperature better because it breathes, while fleece traps heat well but can feel stuffy.

Which is easier to care for?

Fleece. Most fleece throws are machine washable and dry fast. Many wool throws need gentle washing or airing out instead.

Many homes end up with one of each: a wool throw on the sofa and a washable fleece for movie nights, pets and guests.
