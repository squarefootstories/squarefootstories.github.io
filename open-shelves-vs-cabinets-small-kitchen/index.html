Open Shelves vs Cabinets in a Small Kitchen: Which Is Better?

By the Square Foot Stories team · Updated October 10, 2026 · 1 min read · How we write guides

Open shelves are popular in small kitchens because they make walls feel lighter. They aren't right for everyone, though.

Open shelves: the case for them

They make a small kitchen feel larger, keep everyday dishes within reach and cost less than new cabinets.

Open shelves: the trade-offs

Everything is on display, so they need regular tidying and collect dust and grease near the stove.

Cabinets: the case for them

Doors hide mismatched items and keep things clean, and they usually hold more.

Cabinets: the trade-offs

A full wall of upper cabinets can make a small kitchen feel closed in.

Quick verdict: Neat and cook daily? Mix in open shelves. Lots of stuff? Keep cabinets.

What to look for before you buy

Shelf brackets

Check the weight rating and wall type, and confirm your lease allows drilling.

Dishes for open shelves

Matching or coordinated dishes look tidier on display.

Cabinet organizers

Risers and pull-out bins make more of the cabinets you have.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Putting open shelves next to the stove.
  • Overloading shelves.
  • Displaying everything you own.

Questions people ask

Are open shelves practical?

For everyday dishes you use and wash often, yes. For things that sit for weeks, cabinets keep them cleaner.

Can renters add open shelves?

Only if the lease allows drilling. Freestanding shelves are an alternative.

Many small kitchens work best with a mix: cabinets for most storage and one or two open shelves for everyday dishes.
